# Domain: Knowledge

> **Core question: What is knowledge in AppleGo?**
>
> Objects that appear here: Knowledge Graph, Concept, Understanding
> Parent domain: [Environment](./environment.md)

---

## What Knowledge Is

Knowledge is not a collection of facts. Knowledge is not a curriculum. Knowledge is not a list of topics.

**Knowledge is the structured understanding that the learner has built through learning.**

It has shape. It has depth. It has connections. It evolves.

---

## How Knowledge Forms

Knowledge is not uploaded. It is not entered. It is formed through learning.

### Step 1: Encounter

The learner encounters a concept — through reading, watching, conversation, or practice. The concept enters the Workspace.

### Step 2: Initial Understanding

The learner grasps the concept at a surface level. "I know what this means." This understanding is fragile. It can be lost without reinforcement.

### Step 3: Connection

The concept connects to existing knowledge. "This is like X." "This is the opposite of Y." "This explains why Z works." Connections give knowledge stability.

### Step 4: Deepening

The understanding deepens through use. The learner applies the concept. Explains it. Sees it from new angles. The concept becomes internal.

### Step 5: Integration

The concept is fully integrated into the learner's knowledge structure. It is no longer "something I learned." It is "something I know."

---

## Knowledge Structure

Knowledge in AppleGo is not a flat list. It is a structure.

### Concepts

The basic unit. A single idea or topic. "Limit." "Derivative." "TCP handshake."

### Connections

Relationships between concepts.

- "Derivative builds on limits."
- "TCP and UDP are alternative transport protocols."
- "ε-δ definition formalizes limit intuition."

Connections are what make knowledge a network, not a list.

### Clusters

Groups of related concepts that naturally belong together. Clusters emerge over time — they are not predefined categories.

TCP, HTTP, Socket, DNS → the Environment recognizes they form "Network." This is self-organization, not manual filing.

### Depth

Every concept has a depth of understanding — not a score, but a description of what the learner can do:

- Surface: can recognize and describe
- Understanding: can explain in own words
- Application: can use to solve problems
- Connection: can relate to other concepts
- Creation: can build or teach from this knowledge

---

## Knowledge Evolution

Knowledge is not static. It evolves as the learner grows.

### Widening

New concepts are added. The knowledge structure expands.

### Deepening

Existing concepts are understood more deeply. What was "I know the definition" becomes "I can prove theorems using this."

### Reorganization

Connections shift. A concept that seemed isolated now belongs to a cluster. A connection that once seemed important now seems obvious. The structure reorganizes as understanding matures.

### Correction

Misunderstandings are resolved. A concept the learner thought they understood turns out to be wrong. The knowledge structure is corrected — and the correction itself is remembered as a milestone.

---

## Knowledge vs Memory

Knowledge and Memory are distinct domains.

| | Knowledge | Memory |
|---|-----------|--------|
| What it holds | What the learner understands | What happened during learning |
| Shape | Structure (concepts, connections, clusters) | Narrative (events, patterns, stories) |
| How it grows | Deepening, widening, reorganization | Accumulation, pattern recognition, narrative formation |
| Example | "The learner understands limits at the application level" | "The learner struggled with limits on Day 3, understood visually on Day 7, applied them on Day 14" |

Knowledge is the "what." Memory is the "how and when."

Together, they give AppleGo a complete picture of learning: what the learner knows, and how they came to know it.

---

## Knowledge and the Environment

Knowledge is not displayed in a dashboard. It is embedded in the Environment.

- The mindmap shows the current structure of understanding.
- The Canvas reveals connections the learner sees.
- The concepts that keep appearing in Sessions form clusters.
- The companion references knowledge: "You already understand limits. Derivatives build on that."

Knowledge makes the Environment intelligent. The Environment knows what the learner knows — and can shape itself around that understanding.
